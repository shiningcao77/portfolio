01 / AI EVALUATION · HUMAN–AI COMPARISON

Synthetic UAT

Testing whether AI personas hit the same usability friction as real people in a cloud billing task.

Participant names and Google internal materials are omitted.

MY ROLE
UX Researcher & Designer · survey, test protocol, test environment, POC lead
TIMELINE
Sep–Dec 2026
COMPANY/CLIENT
Google Cloud Business Platform IX team · Contract capstone project (Cornell MPS)
SKILLS/TOOLS
Survey design, usability testing, think-aloud analysis, heuristic evaluation, expert interviews, Figma, HTML/CSS
OverviewProblemSolutionImpactOpportunitiesReflection

01 / OVERVIEW

An early usability signal for UX teams.

I helped test whether AI personas can give UX teams an early usability signal. On a team of seven across UX research, design and data science, I wrote the participant survey and test protocol, built the billing test environment, ran and analyzed 9 usability sessions, and led the design of a platform proof of concept for running synthetic tests.

02 / PROBLEM

Do synthetic users get stuck in the same places as people?

Human usability testing takes time to recruit and run. AI personas are faster, but they can be more consistent than real users and miss the confusion that makes findings useful. Three expert interviews said the same: human feedback can't be skipped. So the question became whether synthetic users get stuck in the same places as people.

03 / SOLUTION

Make humans and AI face the same task.

After the pilot showed our first prototype was too basic, I rebuilt the test environment in HTML and CSS on GitHub Pages. Participants now enter through a console-style flow instead of landing on the billing page, and every click is logged for comparison.

Turn people into persona inputs.

I wrote a profile survey scored 0–10, with questions drawn from the data science engine's variables, pilot findings, client feedback and research papers. Ten participants completed it, including the pilot.

Design the platform researchers would use.

I led the POC with three decisions:

  • Live prototypes only, because personas have to actually use the interface to produce behavioral findings.
  • A step-by-step wizard plus an AI assistant for setup. The heuristic evaluation flagged manual setup as slow, and the wizard keeps full control.
  • AI analysis that cites session evidence, because experts warned AI output can be inaccurate.

Expert interviews also led us to add multi-persona selection.

04 / IMPACT

Synthetic runs caught what people did, not why.

Research

9 moderated think-aloud sessions (plus a pilot), 3 interviews with UXR professionals at Google, a heuristic evaluation, an affinity map with five themes, and an issue frequency matrix.

Most common human issue

Finding Budgets (5 of 9 participants, 55.6%), followed by mixing up the budget amount and alert thresholds (3 of 9).

Human vs. synthetic

I compared 240 synthetic runs across three cohorts (students, other backgrounds, FinOps) with the participants they matched. Of 8 human issues, 3 were direct matches (finding Budgets, incomplete service coverage, missing the $800 alert) and 2 were partial matches. 2 were missed or different (lost unsaved draft, extra alert recipients). 1 appeared only in think-aloud (uncertainty and terminology).

Deliverables

Survey, protocol, test environment, Figma POC, heuristic evaluation, handoff documentation.

Proof of concept on one workflow. Only one human FinOps reference, and counts mix participants, runs and saved budgets.

05 / OPPORTUNITIES

Show teams where to look before recruiting people.

Useful today for spotting observable problems, like navigation detours and missed settings, before recruiting people. It can't see confusion that leaves no trace in saved settings, so findings that matter still need real users.

06 / REFLECTION

Matching the setup mattered as much as the personas.

Matching the test setup mattered as much as the personas, so I rebuilt our environment after the pilot. Synthetic runs matched people on what they did but not on why, such as reading a budget as a hard cap or being unsure it saved. With more time I would recruit more people per persona, since FinOps rested on a single participant. I would also test the POC with 5–8 researchers.
