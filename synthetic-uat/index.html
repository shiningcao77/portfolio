Synthetic UAT

Testing whether AI personas hit the same usability friction as real people in a cloud billing task.

01 / OVERVIEW

An early usability signal for UX teams.

I helped test whether AI personas can give UX teams an early usability signal. On a team of seven across UX research, design and data science, I wrote the participant survey and test protocol, built the billing test environment, ran and analyzed usability sessions, and led the design of a platform proof of concept for running synthetic tests.

02 / PROBLEM

Do synthetic users get stuck in the same places as people?

Human usability testing takes time to recruit and run. AI personas are faster, but they can be more consistent than real users and miss the confusion that makes findings useful. Three expert interviews said the same: human feedback can't be skipped. So the question became whether synthetic users get stuck in the same places as people.

03 / SOLUTION

Make humans and AI face the same task.

After the pilot showed our first prototype was too basic, I rebuilt the test environment in HTML and CSS on GitHub Pages. Participants now enter through a console-style flow instead of landing on the billing page, and every click is logged for comparison.

Turn people into persona inputs.

I wrote a profile survey scored 0–10, with questions drawn from the data science engine's variables, pilot findings, client feedback and research papers. Ten participants completed it, including the pilot.

Design the platform researchers would use.

I led the POC with three decisions:

  • Live prototypes only, because personas have to actually use the interface to produce behavioral findings.
  • A step-by-step wizard plus an AI assistant for setup. The heuristic evaluation flagged manual setup as slow, and the wizard keeps full control.
  • AI analysis that cites session evidence, because experts warned AI output can be inaccurate.

Expert interviews also led us to add multi-persona selection.

04 / IMPACT

Where people and synthetic personas aligned—and differed.

Research

9 usability sessions plus a pilot, 3 expert interviews, a heuristic evaluation, think-aloud analysis and an issue frequency matrix.

What people struggled with

Finding the budget page was hardest, with 5 of 9 participants detouring through unrelated billing pages. Alert habits also differed. Some set an $800 budget with several alert levels, while others kept $1,000 with default-style percentages and no $800 warning.

Human vs. synthetic · first comparison

Both struggled most at finding the budget page. 83% of participants and 78% of runs detoured, about 40% of session time.

05 / OPPORTUNITIES

Show teams where to look before recruiting people.

Synthetic testing fits early exploration, showing UX and product teams where to look before recruiting people. Anything that matters still needs confirmation with real users.

06 / REFLECTION

Matching the setup mattered as much as the personas.

Matching the test setup mattered as much as the personas. The comparison is only as fair as the shared environment, which is why I rebuilt ours after the pilot. Synthetic users were good at showing where a group gets stuck, but not at reproducing individual choices like second-guessing a value.

With more time I would recruit more people per persona, run sessions without a moderator to see when people give up, and test the POC itself with 5–8 researchers.
