FIG. 01 — TV & BROADCASTING

The Emmys Redesign

A UX redesign of the official Emmy Awards website, built around a modular template system that brings consistency to a site spanning dozens of award categories.

Location USA
Role UX/UI & Web Redesign
Industry TV & Broadcasting
Year 2025

FIG. 02 — PROBLEM STATEMENT

Three sources of friction

  • 01 Every award category followed a different layout, so the site felt inconsistent from one page to the next.
  • 02 Dense, unstructured pages buried the details visitors actually came for — nominees, winners, category info.
  • 03 A static structure and unclear pathways made it hard to browse, and didn't match the prestige of the Emmys brand.

FIG. 03 — RESEARCH & ANALYSIS

Learning from other award shows

I looked at how the Oscars, Golden Globes, and Tony Awards handle the same problem — organizing a huge amount of category and nominee content — to see what worked and what didn't.

Alongside the competitive review, user feedback pointed to the same conclusion: visitors wanted a browsing experience that felt clear and consistent no matter which category they explored — which shaped the decision to design one unified template rather than one-off pages.

FIG. 04 — IA & WIREFRAME

A modular template system

Rather than designing each award category as its own page, I mapped out an information architecture built on a repeatable template — so the same structure could scale across every category while staying easy to maintain.

FIG. 05 — FINAL DESIGN

Refined for clarity and consistency

The final designs were refined through feedback for usability, accessibility, and responsiveness, so the finished product held up across devices while staying true to the Emmy brand.

FIG. 06 — TAKEAWAYS

What this project confirmed

  • Award sites across the industry share the same gap: no scalable design system.
  • A flexible, template-based framework can hold brand consistency and easy content management at the same time.
