FIG. 01 — GROCERY / E-COMMERCE

Esia

A grocery delivery app designed to simplify online shopping — built as a foundation that could scale from a single local store to a wider market.

Role UX/UI Design Intern
Sector Grocery / E-commerce
Platforms Mobile & Web

FIG. 02 — PROBLEM STATEMENT

Where shoppers were dropping off

  • 01 Carts were getting abandoned before checkout.
  • 02 Checkout itself felt too complex for a quick grocery run.
  • 03 Unclear product information left shoppers unsure of what they were buying.
  • 04 Low trust in the platform, with no same-day delivery to back it up.

Objective — design a modern, user-friendly grocery delivery platform that improves convenience and trust today, while laying the groundwork to scale across multiple stores later.

FIG. 03 — RESEARCH & ANALYSIS

Listening to real grocery shoppers

Surveys and one-on-one interviews surfaced the everyday frustrations that don't show up in a feature list: confusing delivery fees, the letdown of an out-of-stock item, trouble finding specific products, and an app that just doesn't fit a busy schedule.

FIG. 04 — USER PERSONA & FLOW

Designing around the Busy Professional

The primary persona — a Busy Professional balancing time, budget, and a preference for fresh, sustainable products — anchored every decision that followed. Mapping their journey, from product search to checkout, made it clear exactly where friction needed to be removed.

FIG. 05 — WIREFRAME & VISUAL DESIGN

From wireframe to a working interface

Low-fidelity wireframes let me test the structure of search, browsing, and checkout early, before layering in the final visual design across both mobile and web.

FIG. 06 — TAKEAWAYS

What this project taught me

  • Prioritize user needs over extra features — the app stays simple, intuitive, and efficient.
  • Clear, consistent communication in design builds user trust and confidence.
  • Iterative prototyping makes it possible to test ideas quickly and fold in feedback fast.
  • Business goals and user experience aren't opposites — promotions and engagement features can work with the shopping flow, not against it.
